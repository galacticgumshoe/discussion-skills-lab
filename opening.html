SECTION 01 / 06

How a Discussion Round Works

Every 60-minute Discussion round includes two kinds of contributions. Strong participants know when the group needs structure—and when it needs ideas and evidence.

01 · STRUCTURE

Procedural

Provides structure, organization, and focus. It helps the group work effectively without directly developing solutions.

  • Clarify the task
  • Set an agenda
  • Manage time and focus
  • Organize the discussion
02 · CONTENT

Substantive

Develops ideas, introduces evidence, evaluates possibilities, and moves the group toward solving the task.

  • Share research and evidence
  • Explain causes or effects
  • Evaluate solutions
  • Synthesize viewpoints

SECTION 02 / 06

What Happens at the Start of a Round?

A Discussion round lasts 60 minutes. Its opening gives the group a shared understanding of the task and a direction for working together. Open each step to learn more.

The opening sequence

The task may arrive on paper or electronically. Wait until the judge and participants are ready before beginning.

Read the task silently. The group usually asks someone to read it once aloud. Use this time to identify WHO, WHAT, HOW, WHERE, and useful SEARCH TERMS.

Discuss what the task means. Reach a shared understanding of what the group must accomplish.

After clarification, develop an agenda or propose a consensus question based on the task.

Students typically spend approximately five minutes locating useful evidence in their research materials.

Share essential background and move into substantive discussion. Re-clarify only if an important uncertainty remains.

Order matters: clarify what the task requires before setting an agenda.

SECTION 03 / 06

Decoding a Complex Task

Five questions turn a dense task into a practical plan.

WHO

What role is the group playing? What authority does it have?

WHAT

What central problem is the group trying to solve?

HOW

What actions or final deliverables are required?

WHERE

Where does the problem occur, and where will solutions be implemented?

SEARCH TERMS

Which keywords, organizations, and concepts will locate useful research?

Remember: WHAT identifies the problem. HOW identifies what the group must do about it.

Analyze the Task

Select a category to highlight relevant language and reveal its meaning.

Mental health issues have long been stigmatized in the United States and most of the world. While this problem has decreased over time, it is still far too prevalent for many who suffer from these illnesses. You are members of the American School Counselor Association, meeting at their national convention to discuss more active methods to combat mental illness. Your goal is to examine what effects the stigmatization of mental illness still has on K-12 students and craft a plan which can be implemented by counselors at their schools to support these children.

Choose a category to reveal an answer.

Search terms are drawn from concepts throughout the task, rather than one continuous phrase.

SECTION 04 / 06

Make Sure Everyone Understands the Task

Clarifications happen after reading the task and before discussing solutions. The goal is shared understanding—not an early argument about which solution wins.

Clarify four essentials

✓ The group's assigned role

✓ The central problem

✓ The actions required

✓ The final product or deliverable

Participate constructively: ask useful questions, listen carefully, and help resolve uncertainty before the group chooses its direction.

Decision point

“Let's spend 20 minutes discussing possible counseling programs.”

SECTION 05 / 06

Give the Discussion Direction

Once the task is clear, the group can choose a roadmap, a destination, or both.

↗

Agenda = Roadmap

An agenda organizes stages and may suggest flexible time limits. Build it from the task's requirements, especially HOW—not before clarification.

  1. Share relevant background research.
  2. Examine major effects of stigma on K-12 students.
  3. Develop components of a counselor plan.
  4. Agree on recommendations.
  5. Summarize the final plan.
◎

Consensus Question = Destination

A consensus question defines what the group should agree it has accomplished by the end. It joins WHAT with HOW and helps maintain focus without a rigid schedule.

“By the end of this discussion, will the group agree that we have crafted a plan counselors could implement in K-12 schools to support students affected by mental health stigma?”

Build a Consensus Question

Write the problem and required accomplishment. Your draft is yours to edit; compare it with the model rather than expecting an automatic grade.

Model: By the end of this discussion, will the group agree that we have crafted a plan counselors could implement in K-12 schools to support students affected by mental health stigma?

Check your consensus question

After writing your own question, compare it with the model. Confirm that it identifies the problem and the accomplishment. This is a self-check, not automated grading.

SECTION 06 / 06

From Structure to Substance

Research and background sharing move the group from organizing its work to developing useful ideas.

01 · Research

Use approximately five minutes to locate evidence. Search with task terms, the season's research outline, and sources tied to the deliverable. Prepare verbal citations: publisher, publication date, and author or title when available.

02 · Background

Share useful definitions, essential context, and evidence relevant to the discussion. Help everyone start from a common understanding.

03 · Re-clarification

If research exposes a real misunderstanding, resolve it briefly. Avoid repeating clarifications the group has already settled.
